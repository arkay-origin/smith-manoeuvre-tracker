Amortization schedule

HELOC

Multiple lines supported (multi-lender setups). Draws are tagged by purpose so investment principal and capitalized interest never blur together — that split is what keeps your deduction clean.

How this tool calculates HELOC interest — read before relying on the numbers

Simple daily accrual, actual/365. Interest is accrued daily on the deductible balance at the rate in force, with no internal compounding and no leap-year adjustment. Most Canadian HELOCs accrue daily and charge monthly, so this is close — but it is an estimate.

Compounding is captured by you, not assumed. If you let interest capitalize, record it as a Capitalized interest draw. The tool then compounds it exactly as it actually happened rather than guessing a frequency. If you don't record those draws, the estimate will run low.

Principal payments split pro-rata. On a line holding both deductible and non-deductible balances, a payment reduces each in proportion to its share. That reflects the CRA's general position on mixed-use borrowing — you normally cannot direct repayments at the non-deductible portion first. If your situation supports different tracing, the figures here won't match your filing position.

Your lender's statements govern. Reconcile against them before filing.

Investments

ACB uses the CRA average-cost method. Distributions with return of capital reduce ACB; reinvested (phantom) distributions increase it. Splits supported, including fractional handling.

Tax

These are estimates, not statements. Ledger figures use simple daily accrual (actual/365) and split mixed-line payments pro-rata. Reconcile against your lender's annual interest statement before filing — where they differ, the statement wins.

Deductible interest = interest attributable to draws tagged Investment or Capitalized interest on those draws. Interest goes on Line 22100 (carrying charges) of your T1. Mixed-use draws are excluded — keep them out of your SM line entirely.

Record-keeping guide — reference only, not included in the printed package

    
What the CRA actually wants
      
A clean, traceable flow: HELOC draw → dedicated chequing (optional but tidy) → non-registered investment account. Never mix personal spending into the SM credit line. Keep statements at least six years. One experienced filer's approach: once a year download 12 months of HELOC interest, SM chequing, and SM investment statements — that alone satisfies most reviews.

    
Capitalizing interest
      
Borrowing from the HELOC to pay the HELOC's own interest keeps the strategy cash-flow neutral, and that borrowed interest remains deductible if the underlying draws were for investment. Track it as its own draw type (this tool does) so the split survives an audit.

    
Return of capital (ROC)
      
ROC distributions reduce your ACB, and — if spent rather than reinvested or paid onto the investment loan — reduce the deductible portion of the loan. High-ROC ETFs create bookkeeping drag; many SM practitioners prefer dividend payers or low-ROC funds for that reason.

    
Mixed-use contamination
      
One personal draw on an investment-only line forces you to prorate every interest payment forever after. Use a separate line or sub-account for anything personal. If contamination already happened, prorate honestly and document the math.

    
Superficial loss rule
      
Selling at a loss and rebuying the identical security within 30 days (before or after), in any account you or your spouse control, denies the loss. The tool flags same-account cases only.
